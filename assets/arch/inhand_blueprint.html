Executive summary

One router, three internet paths, and a charger Wi-Fi network that never drops.

The InHand IR302 cannot serve local Wi-Fi and use Wi-Fi as its own uplink at the same time. Offloading charger Wi-Fi to a dedicated outdoor access point frees the router to fail over across cellular, wired Ethernet, and site Wi-Fi without ever taking the chargers offline.

Deep dive

Three uplinks, one failover policy

The IR302 holds all three WAN paths in a single priority list with ICMP dead-link detection. Any path can be primary; the other two stand by.

One SIM that shops the whole radio market

A smart multi-carrier SIM presents one profile to the IR302 but holds credentials for several operator networks. When the serving network stops answering, the SIM re-attaches to the next-best operator on its own — no second physical SIM, no second contract, no site visit.

Carrier pool · North America

AT&T · T-Mobile · Verizon · Rogers · Bell · Telus, selected by signal quality and local policy.

Recovery behaviour

Attach to a new PLMN in roughly 30–90 s. OCPP queues transactions locally and replays on reconnect.

Commercial

One pooled data plan across the fleet, one bill, one portal. Dual-SIM builds need two of everything.

Why not dual physical SIM

The IR302 has two SIM slots, but each slot is a separate account, APN and renewal date — and a swap is a truck roll.

Trade-offs

  • +Works on any site with no IT dependency — nothing to request from the venue.
  • +Survives a whole-carrier regional outage, which a single-carrier SIM cannot.
  • +Private APN and static IP keep the OCPP endpoint off the public internet.
  • −Recurring data cost per site; latency of 40–70 ms versus 8–15 ms on fibre.
  • −Underground or shielded garages may need an external antenna on the LTE ports.

The best path when the cable already exists

Where the property already has structured cabling to the parking area, the IR302's FE0 port takes a hand-off from the building network. It is the cheapest bandwidth on site and the most stable — and it is the path that most often disappears when a landscaper finds the conduit.

Hand-off

DHCP or static from the venue. Ask for a dedicated VLAN so charger traffic is isolated from tenant traffic.

Firewall asks

Outbound TCP 443 (OCPP-J over WSS) and NTP. No inbound rules required.

Distance limit

100 m of copper per run. Beyond that, fibre with a media converter, or fall back to cellular.

Failure signature

Hard link-loss is detected in under a second; a "grey" upstream outage is caught by the ICMP probe in ~5 s.

Trade-offs

  • +No recurring data charge, no radio planning, no antenna.
  • +Symmetric bandwidth for firmware pushes and remote diagnostics.
  • −Trenching or conduit is the single largest line item on a retrofit.
  • −You inherit the venue's outages, maintenance windows and IT change control.
  • −FE0 is 100 Mbps — fine for OCPP, not a gigabit hand-off.
radio conflictsolved by AP offload

The IR302 has one radio — it cannot be an AP and a client at once

Putting the router's Wi-Fi into station mode to pick up the venue SSID takes the internal access point down. Every charger associated to the router's SSID is dropped at that moment. The fix is not a firmware setting — it is a second box.

Without the outdoor AP

Radio mode collision

  1. 01Chargers associate to the IR302's own SSID in AP mode. Everything works.
  2. 02Operator switches the router to STA mode to use venue Wi-Fi as the uplink.
  3. 03The single 2.4 GHz radio leaves AP service. The charger SSID stops being broadcast.
  4. 04All chargers de-associate. Sessions cannot be authorised; the site is dark until someone reverts the mode.

RESULT · site Wi-Fi is unusable as an uplink. Only 2 of 3 WAN paths remain.

With the Festa F41 outdoor AP

Access layer separated from the WAN layer

  1. 01Chargers associate to the F41's SSID, powered over Ethernet from the switch below the router.
  2. 02The IR302's own radio is now free — put it in STA mode permanently, or disable it outright.
  3. 03The router switches between Ethernet, venue Wi-Fi and cellular as conditions change.
  4. 04Charger association is untouched by any WAN event — the AP never changes state.

RESULT · 3 of 3 WAN paths available, plus IP65 coverage across the whole lot.

Coverage bonus. The IR302 is an IP30 indoor-rated device in an enclosure — its internal antenna was never going to cover a parking lot. The F41 is IP65 with 6 kV surge protection and mounts on the pole or wall where the chargers actually are, so the offload buys range and weather rating as well as radio independence.
