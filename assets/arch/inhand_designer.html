Every source, and what it costs you

Four ways to get an IR302 online. Any combination can be held in one priority list with ICMP dead-link detection — but only one of them changes what hardware the site needs.

multi-IMSICat 4 · 150 Mbps DL2 physical SIM slots

The IR302 has two SIM slots. The question is what you put in them.

A single smart multi-carrier SIM presents one profile to the router but holds credentials for several operator networks and re-attaches on its own. Two ordinary carrier SIMs give you the same physical redundancy with two of every commercial artefact — and a truck roll whenever one has to change.

Trade-offs · cellular in general

  • +Installs with no venue IT dependency — nothing to request from the landlord.
  • +The only path that survives a venue-wide network outage.
  • +Private APN with a static IP keeps the OCPP endpoint off the public internet.
  • −Recurring cost per site, and 40–70 ms latency against 8–15 ms on fibre.
  • −Underground or shielded garages need external antennas on the LTE ports.
  • !A single ordinary SIM is not redundancy — one carrier outage takes the site down.
FE0 · WAN10/100 Mbpslowest latency

The best path when the cable already exists

Where the property already has structured cabling to the parking area, the IR302's FE0 port takes a hand-off from the building network. It is the cheapest bandwidth on site and the most stable — and it is the path that most often disappears when a landscaper finds the conduit.

Hand-off

DHCP or static from the venue. Ask for a dedicated VLAN so charger traffic is isolated from tenant traffic.

Firewall asks

Outbound TCP 443 (OCPP-J over WSS) and NTP. No inbound rules required.

Distance limit

100 m of copper per run. Beyond that, fibre with a media converter, or fall back to cellular.

Failure signature

Hard link-loss is detected in under a second; a "grey" upstream outage is caught by the ICMP probe in ~5 s.

Trade-offs

  • +No recurring data charge, no radio planning, no antenna.
  • +Symmetric bandwidth for firmware pushes and remote diagnostics.
  • −Trenching or conduit is the single largest line item on a retrofit.
  • −You inherit the venue's outages, maintenance windows and change control.
  • −FE0 is 100 Mbps — fine for OCPP, not a gigabit hand-off.
radio conflictsolved by AP offload+$100 hardware

The IR302 has one radio — it cannot be an AP and a client at once

Putting the router's Wi-Fi into station mode to pick up the venue SSID takes the internal access point down. Every charger associated to the router's SSID is dropped at that moment. This is why enabling the Wi-Fi uplink in the designer above forces the outdoor AP into the build — and why turning the uplink off again lets you delete it and save the money.

Wi-Fi uplink without the outdoor AP

Radio mode collision

  1. 01Chargers associate to the IR302's own SSID in AP mode. Everything works.
  2. 02Operator switches the router to STA mode to use venue Wi-Fi as the uplink.
  3. 03The single 2.4 GHz radio leaves AP service. The charger SSID stops being broadcast.
  4. 04All chargers de-associate. Sessions cannot be authorised until someone reverts the mode.

PROVE IT · enable the Wi-Fi uplink, make it rank 1, then throw "Outdoor AP fails" in the simulator.

With the Festa F41 outdoor AP

Access layer separated from the WAN layer

  1. 01Chargers associate to the F41's SSID, powered over Ethernet from the switch below the router.
  2. 02The IR302's own radio is now free — put it in STA mode permanently, or disable it outright.
  3. 03The router switches between every configured uplink as conditions change.
  4. 04Charger association is untouched by any WAN event — the AP never changes state.

RESULT · the Wi-Fi uplink becomes available, plus IP65 coverage across the whole lot.

When you can drop the AP. If the design uses only cellular and wired Ethernet, the router's own radio can serve the chargers and the F41 plus the PoE switch come out of the BOM — about $100 per site. Weigh that against what you lose: the IR302 is IP30 and lives in an enclosure, so charger coverage is then limited to whatever its internal antenna reaches, and adding a venue Wi-Fi uplink later becomes a site visit rather than a config change.
